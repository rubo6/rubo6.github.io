3 November 2025 was my first day at Mercado Libre. A few weeks earlier I had done the first job interview of my life, and I want to write down how it felt before memory turns it into something more heroic than it was.

## Two interviews, no prior reference

The process was shorter than I feared. The first interview mixed two things: getting to know me as a person and asking technical questions and questions about my school projects. I talked about Data Sources, databases and what I had done at DataLab. The second was with the person who would then be one of my managers; today, after a change of area, I stayed with my current manager, Diego.

What I did not know at the time is that I was competing with other candidates from ITAM, UNAM and Tec. Learning that afterwards helped me understand that, if they chose me, it was for something concrete and not luck.

## The nerves

I was very nervous. I had no reference for what an interview looks like from the inside: whether to talk a lot or a little, whether saying "I don't know" is fine, whether asking questions signals interest or insecurity. What saved me was talking about things I had actually done. Whenever the conversation reached a real project the nerves dropped by themselves, because there was no role to play there.

## What I would do differently

- Prepare three short project stories, each with a problem, a decision and a result. That is what gets asked in the end, however it is phrased.
- Say "I don't know, but here is how I would find out" instead of circling the question.
- Ask about the team and the day-to-day. Today I work remotely with people in Mexico, Brazil and Argentina, in Spanish, and I would have liked to know that earlier.

## What I take with me

That the first interview of your life is won beforehand, by doing things you can talk about. And that nerves do not disappear: they become manageable when you have something true to tell.
